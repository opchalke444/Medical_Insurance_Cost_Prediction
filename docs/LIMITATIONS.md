# Academic & Real-World Limitations
### MedCost.AI — Medical Insurance Cost Prediction

---

## 1. Executive Summary
MedCost.AI is developed as an academic machine learning research and demonstration project. It maps basic demographic indicators (`age`, `sex`, `bmi`, `children`, `smoker`, `region`) to annual insurance expenditures (`charges`). 

While the models achieve high predictive correlation ($R^2 \approx 0.8185$ for the selected KNN model), this document establishes the critical academic, clinical, and economic boundaries of the system.

---

## 2. Key Academic & Domain Limitations

### 2.1 Sample Size & Geographic Granularity
- **Sample Scale:** The models were trained on the benchmark US Medical Cost dataset ($N=1,337$ unique records after deduplication). While sufficient for classroom statistical benchmarking, this is orders of magnitude smaller than enterprise actuarial datasets comprising millions of covered lives.
- **Regional Quadrants:** Geographical residency is aggregated into only four broad quadrants (`northeast`, `northwest`, `southeast`, `southwest`). In actual healthcare economics, pricing disparities depend heavily on state insurance regulations, hospital network agreements, and municipal zip-code level cost-of-living indices.

### 2.2 Absence of Clinical & Diagnostic Markers
The dataset intentionally isolates demographic and lifestyle proxies, omitting all clinical diagnostic history:
- **Biometric & Lab Panels:** Missing critical biomarkers such as blood glucose (HbA1c), lipid profiles (LDL/HDL), resting blood pressure, and cardiovascular screening.
- **Diagnostic History:** Lacks diagnostic codes (ICD-10) for chronic conditions (e.g., Type 2 Diabetes, hypertension, autoimmune conditions).
- **Prescription & Utilization:** Lacks prior year hospitalization counts, emergency room visits, and ongoing prescription therapy tiers.
In commercial underwriting, clinical lab values are the primary determinant of individual morbidity risks.

### 2.3 Heteroskedastic Residuals & High-Claim Volatility
Subgroup error analysis reveals pronounced heteroskedasticity (non-constant error variance):
- **Non-Smoker Cohort ($n=208$ in test set):** Mean actual expenditure is $\$8,202.99$, with a stable $\text{MAE} \approx \$2,636.09$.
- **Smoker Cohort ($n=60$ in test set):** Mean actual expenditure is $\$35,311.26$, with an elevated $\text{MAE} \approx \$7,085.22$ ($2.6\times$ higher).
The model accurately captures the baseline penalty of smoking, but cannot discern between a smoker who visits an outpatient clinic vs. one who undergoes open-heart surgery.

### 2.4 Demographic Dataset Temporal Stagnation
- The underlying benchmark dataset reflects historical healthcare cost levels. Due to medical inflation, hospital consolidation, and changing pharmaceutical costs, nominal USD expenditure predictions do not track current calendar-year medical costs without inflation adjustment indices.

---

## 3. Disclaimers & Intended Use Policy
1. **Educational & Research Scope:** MedCost.AI is designed strictly for academic evaluation, algorithm benchmarking, and viva voce presentation.
2. **Not an Actuarial Quote:** Predictions generated by the API do not constitute formal insurance underwriting, premium quotes, or legal insurance policies.
3. **Zero Medical Advice:** Estimates must never be used to guide personal healthcare decisions, clinical diagnoses, or medical treatment planning.
